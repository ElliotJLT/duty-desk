Duty desk Talk to Elliot

How it works

Five short panels. What the job is, what the desk does, who it talks to, the rules it keeps, and why it gets better every time a trip goes sideways.

1 · The job

When a trip changes, someone has to get every guest back to the group.

  • Many small-group operators sell land only. Guests book their own flights, and the operator knows them only from the pre-trip form.
  • When a flight is cancelled, the operator doesn't rebook it. They find out the guest's new arrival, move the airport pickup with the local agent (the DMC), tell the hotel, and decide where the guest joins the group.
  • That runs across WhatsApp, email and one person's memory, for every guest, until each one is with the group.

2 · What the desk does

It does the chasing. People make the calls.

  • It never books, pays or messages a guest on its own. Every change goes to the person who can sign it off.
  • It keeps a live list of who isn't sorted yet, and it doesn't let anyone drop off the list quietly.

3 · Who's in the room

Your team works in Slack. Everyone else stays where they already are.

  • Local agents and trip leaders won't adopt another tool for one operator. So the desk goes to them: WhatsApp and email, the channels they already answer.
  • Guests message the way they always do. Their replies land in the same room as everything else.
  • Nobody outside your team installs anything or learns anything new.

That's the adoption bet, and the hardest part to prove. It depends on whether local agents answer by name when asked. The next test has to check that with a real one.

4 · The rules

Seven rules, each one a mistake it won't make.

Each rule has a test that runs against the replay. If the rule breaks, the test fails.

Start from the trip listOtherwise you check the French trip and miss the Spanish one whose flights cross France.
Good news closes nothingOtherwise "strike's off" becomes "you're all good", six hours before the flight is cancelled.
Only the right person confirmsOtherwise "all sorted 👍" from a local agent ticks off five transfers nobody named.
The right person decidesOtherwise changes happen that nobody with authority agreed to.
Say only what's knownOtherwise guests get promises you then have to take back.
Everything has an owner and a deadlineOtherwise a quiet guest stays quiet until someone notices an empty seat.
Keep disagreements visibleOtherwise two reports with different figures get blended into one that neither source said.
5 · How it compounds

Every disruption leaves a record. The next one starts from it.

  • Fewer unknowns. Guests with missing flight details get chased before the next strike, not during it.
  • Better deadlines. It learns which local agents answer in an hour and which need chasing by name, so deadlines match reality.
  • A playbook that improves. The review after each incident turns "what we had to work out" into a rule for next time.
  • A record you can show. Who knew what, when, and who decided. That's the duty-of-care trail insurers and boards ask for, written as the work happens.

This panel is how it's designed to work. None of it has been measured with a real operator yet.
