Duty desk Talk to Elliot

How it works

Six short panels. What the job is, what the desk does, who it talks to, the rules it keeps, and why it gets better every time a trip goes sideways.

1 · The job

When a trip changes, someone has to get every guest back to the group.

  • Many small-group operators sell land only. Guests book their own flights, and the operator knows them only from the pre-trip form.
  • When a flight is cancelled, the operator doesn't rebook it. They find out the guest's new arrival, move the airport pickup with the local partner (often called a DMC), tell the hotel, and decide where the guest joins the group.
  • That runs across WhatsApp, email and one person's memory, for every guest, until each one is with the group.

2 · What the desk does

It does the chasing. People make the calls.

  • It never books, pays or messages a guest on its own. Every change goes to the person who can sign it off.
  • It keeps a live list of who isn't sorted yet, and it doesn't let anyone drop off the list quietly.

3 · Who's in the room

Your team works in Slack. Everyone else stays where they already are.

  • Local partners and trip leaders work with many operators and shouldn't need another app for each. So the desk meets them where they already are: WhatsApp and email.
  • Guests message the way they always do. Their replies land in the same room as everything else.
  • Nobody outside your team installs anything or learns anything new.

This is the part we most want to get right with real local partners, so it fits how they already work rather than adding to it.

4 · The rules

Seven habits it keeps, on every trip.

Each rule has a test that runs against the replay. If the rule breaks, the test fails.

Start from the guest listIt checks each guest's own flight, so a trip in Spain whose flights pass over France isn't missed.
Good news is checked, not assumedWhen a strike is called off, guests stay on the list until their own flights are confirmed.
The right person confirmsA friendly "all sorted 👍" gets a quick follow-up asking which guests it covers, and the list stays exact.
People decideChanges to a trip and messages to guests go to the person on your team who can approve them.
Say only what's knownGuest updates say what's confirmed and what isn't, so nobody has to take a promise back.
Everyone has an ownerEvery open question has someone looking after it and a time to check back, so a quiet guest still gets a call.
Keep both sides of a storyWhen two reports disagree, both are kept with their times, so nobody acts on a figure neither source gave.
5 · How it compounds

Every disruption leaves a record. The next one starts from it.

  • Fewer unknowns. Guests with missing flight details get chased before the next strike, not during it.
  • Better deadlines. It learns how each partner likes to work and how quickly they usually reply, so its reminders fit.
  • A playbook that improves. The review after each incident turns "what we had to work out" into a rule for next time.
  • A record you can show. Who knew what, when, and who decided. That's the duty-of-care trail insurers and boards ask for, written as the work happens.

6 · Under the hood

Where the AI does the work, and where it doesn't.

  • AI reads. Guests, partners and leaders write however they like: "landing Tues morning on the easyJet", "all sorted 👍". A language model turns each message into who, what and when, and shows what it read.
  • AI drafts. Updates for guests, requests for local partners, and the summary for whoever's on call.
  • Rules decide. Who's still open, who confirmed what, and when something escalates are fixed rules, so the same message always gets the same answer and can be checked.
  • People approve. Anything a guest sees, and any change to a trip, until you choose to let routine messages go on their own.

The rules have 23 automated checks against this replay. How accurately it reads messages will be measured on each operator's own messages during a trial, before anything goes out unapproved.

What's real

A real strike, a made-up operator.

  • Real: every news item in the story. The strike notice, the withdrawal, the cuts at Nice and Lyon, the flights over France. Each links to its source and appears when it was published.
  • Made up: Fernway Travel, its staff and guests, the local partners and trip leaders, the airlines "Solent Air" and "Brightwing", and every message between them.
